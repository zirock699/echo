# Echo Studio pilot cards and observation sheets

Use these materials to compare the proposed Practice entry, preparation of a small reference, and recall on a later day. The tasks use invented details. Each comparison can end with the current player, a card, or no further work. [Transformation roadmap](roadmap.md) · [Independent review](independent-review.md) · [Learning rationale](product-learning.md)

## Prepare the cards

1. Choose one comparison and decide what would make a difference to the owner. Copy or print only that section. Set a similar time of day, opportunity and practice window across routes. Allow silence, a pause or an exit at any time. Use the [optional pre-call card](../product/speaking-prompt-handoff.md#e4-f1-and-e4-f2--try-a-comfortable-opening-off-app) if gentle preparation or rest is the day's aim.
2. Have someone other than the speaker cut out and keep the story cards, the delayed cards, and the route order. Give the speaker each new card only at its scheduled moment. A willing first listener gets just the observation questions below. Keep the route, card and key out of their sight until their first account is complete. A helper can prepare sealed cards for an owner who will practise without a listener.
3. If the owner has already read a card, use it to rehearse procedure, then replace it with the private variant rule below. Keep unused delayed cards unexposed. Existing Home and Practice prototypes reuse the same on-screen stories. Count a repeated screen as a repeat exposure and use a different story on paper when comparing what the owner says.
4. Keep a small paper sheet privately. Store route, card code and observation categories if helpful. Leave names, real workplace details, response text, recordings and health information out. A silent plan, skipped occasion, missing observation and an unclear response are distinct. Repeat with a new card after a missed occasion if the owner chooses.

**Private variant rule.** A helper makes short, unused versions with one expected arrangement, one replacement and one action for a named role such as a teammate or organiser. Use ordinary locations, short days or times and familiar objects. Keep the same number of facts, similar words and a comparable action across routes. Never copy a later card's words, numbers or situation into the reference or practice card. Where a measure depends on fixed words, change both alternatives together so either could reasonably be the listener's mistaken detail. The helper assigns and seals the variants before seeing outcomes. If the owner has seen the whole pack and has no helper, use self-written new situations as practice and mark novelty uncertain.

The pack supplies authored text and blank sheets. Model suitability, clearance, physical playback and learning results are open decisions.

## T1 · Find a useful start

Compare the routes below on a phone first. Use clean, disposable empty and prepared states. Keep the same prepared lesson and phrase, Ready or unavailable state, theme and input method in comparable runs. Reset before a new job. Begin with the result the person wants. Avoid naming the button or where to tap until the discovery observation has ended.

Make the existing speaking prompt easy to see beside Add or Resume and give the existing transfer cue a visible action after a chosen reference. Preserve the current destinations and Library. Use the approved click-through if available. With a paper sketch, ask where they would tap and hand them the corresponding unmodified prompt or reference panel. **Give one job at a time.** Rotate the order of the interfaces over comparable opportunities. When evaluating the prototype, include both a direct start and a reference reached through Library.

Fill this out before looking for a new recording. If a prepared favourite already supplies the chosen delivery in this context and is dependable, use it and close the sourcing test. A nearby favourite with a different register or contrast can remain an explicitly unmatched comparison. Choose another target if it would be misleading to compare it.

**Unrecorded candidate script.** “We can send the draft by Friday.” The listener expects the final version. Give them the correction in an ordinary speaking voice, making *draft* easy to hear. Keep the performer’s actual phrasing in the final transcript if it differs. A new take is only worthwhile if this type of contrast fills the gap the owner recorded above.

### Compare text, favourite and whole take

Before the first route, make a short unfamiliar baseline with the same kind of delivery check. The helper can use “We can bring the parcel on Thursday” and privately choose whether the parcel or the day was mistaken. Try one of each route in the first round, then a new order on later comparable occasions. An example is text → favourite → native, then favourite → native → text. Draw a different practice card each time. Keep the same goal, amount of preparation time, speaker effort and end task. A roughly two-minute window suits this small comparison. Set a comfortable duration before starting and use it for all routes. The window measures comparison time. The speaker can stop earlier.

- **Text** uses only the printed situation. Plan or try the intended emphasis once.
- **Favourite** uses the card and the prepared reference with a comparable emphasis. Hear the reference, notice the chosen feature and try the intention once. Log any mismatch.
- **Native** uses the card and the cleared whole take on explicit Play in the ordinary device player. Hear it, notice the chosen feature and try the intention once.

Put away the card and any model, then give the update in your own words. The speaking job on each practice card is to tell a colleague what they can expect and the day. The colleague is mistaken about the item, so make the item clear.

## T5 · Try a different message before replay

### Decide what to observe

Choose one target before the unfamiliar baseline. The default is first-hearing communication of a changed fact and the action to take while waiting. If the owner also wants to test a particular way of retrieving an expression from intention, privately choose it before allocating routes. “In the meantime” is one possible expression if the chosen reference actually supplies it. Each card leaves room for an apt paraphrase. The listener need only report the changed fact and action. Observe the target expression separately with the listener or speaker when appropriate.

Have a prepared short phrase with the chosen contrast or useful expression. Use that same reference in both conditions. Agree a comfortable window, such as 90 seconds. Set a consistent delay, such as the following day, and use roughly the same delay after each practice. Use an unaffected timer or paper notes away from the Echo UI if helpful. Keep the owner free to finish early and label unequal windows.

1. Take the unfamiliar baseline before playing the model. Give one baseline card privately, let the speaker read and put it down, then ask for one update to the listener. Withhold the target wording and any first-listener feedback until the baseline is recorded. If an advanced speaker already completes two different baseline examples easily, agree a more informative aim before the comparison. For example, add one short condition to the action on *all* subsequent cards and take a fresh baseline, or focus on voluntary unaided recall of a personally relevant expression. Avoid raising difficulty only after seeing one route's results.
2. Start each practice with the same listen-once and try-once sequence. For **Replay**, use the remaining practice window to replay and reproduce the model. For **New message**, replace that remaining repetition with one private change card and one independently worded update. Allow silent planning and self-correction in the same available time. Stop the model before the new turn. Put all material away afterward.
3. Try four practice occasions if useful, two of each route, with a varied order such as Replay → New message → New message → Replay or its reverse. At the next visit after each one, give an unused delayed card **before any Replay or model reminder**, then do that day's practice if planned. A baseline, four practice windows and four later probes fit five visits. Draw and assign the story order in advance. Do not reuse practice or phone-check prompts in this test. Note actual interval and any intervening practice. Delay or skip a pairing where exposure or opportunity makes it incomparable.
4. Give the listener the same two initial questions each time. Wait for their first answer before offering a single neutral clarification. Keep score and coaching out of the speaking attempt. With no listener, the speaker can privately note whether they needed the cue or retrieved the expression. Leave listener understanding unobserved.

### First-listener slip

The listener hears one reply and records their own answer before seeing the card or key. They can ask for clarification after giving that first account.

> **What changed?** ___
>
> **What would you do next?** ___
>
> **Optional one clarification.** “Could you clarify what you’d like me to do?” or “Could you clarify when it will happen?” Note any different answer after that reply. ___

Facilitator records each first-heard fact and action as **recovered**, **partly recovered**, **unclear** or **unobserved**. Note the after-clarification result separately. Accept an equivalent action or wording. If the listener sees the card, knows the intended answer or hears the model, label their response informed. Speaker can optionally note whether the selected expression came unaided, an apt alternative came unaided, help was needed or retrieval was unobserved. For T5, count a recurring advantage on unfamiliar tasks and an ordinary voluntary return as a reason to consider a small optional cue. Better copied lines, one good response, short setup or subjective ease alone answer narrower questions. If replay and the cue tie, the baseline is at ceiling, comparisons are missing or paper is enough, defer software work or narrow the test. If a cue adds pressure or speaking feels strained, stop and use silence, rest or an ordinary alternative. The comparisons are a decision aid for this owner. They do not estimate a general learning effect or support a clinical claim. [Existing E1–E4 outcomes and verification](../product/speaking-prompt-handoff.md#outcomes-and-evidence)
