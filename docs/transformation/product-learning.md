# Echo Studio for the next conversation

**Product strategy and learning investigation · `work` at `2b9b8fe` · 25 November 2026**

Echo should make it easy for an advanced speaker to practise something they want to say in the next conversation. A carefully timed reference remains one of its strongest tools. A useful session can also start without one, or move from an interesting line into a new message with a real intention. The proposed product serves both starts, keeps the media player available, and helps the owner find out what carries over. The first decision is whether that change in the practice itself helps. E1 and E2 make the core comparison possible today. [Speaking practice handoff](../product/speaking-prompt-handoff.md)

## Who the product is for

The initial user is the owner, an advanced English speaker who works mainly from home and values the sound of particular British speakers. They report being able to copy a model more readily than finding their own words and delivery under conversational demand. They want to be clearer, more expressive and more comfortable when speaking, and have limited time and appetite for sourcing and timing clips. These are reported needs. The owner’s precise sound targets, preferred voices, baseline on fresh tasks, useful practice frequency and appetite for listener feedback remain open. The product can serve this learner well before considering a broader market or an instructional programme.

On a quiet evening the owner opens a favourite interview, hears a speaker qualify a commitment, and repeats one line. Before a call the next day, the owner wants to explain a date change, say what is confirmed and ask for a next action. A ready cue can start that turn immediately. After a reference-based session, a different fictional update gives the owner a chance to make the same distinction without the wording. A later cue before playback tests whether the phrasing or delivery is available in a different context. A willing partner can play the colleague and ask an unexpected question. The owner can stop after one useful response on any route.

The owner can begin by continuing a chosen reference or speaking from a ready cue. A user who comes to listen can stay with the player. A user preparing an update can start from its intended meaning. The owner can focus on a word people mishear, a detail to bring forward, a useful expression, or an idea to explain to a different audience. On a tired day a silent plan, listening or leaving is a complete choice. Use a single prompt, a quiet resume position and a clear exit. Let repeat visits arise from a useful opportunity and voices the owner likes before testing reminders, daily plans or a taxonomy of skills.

## What the earlier work established

The engineering audit and remediation addressed integrity, transport, accessibility, persistence and reliability that any new experience needs. The prior product specialist traced core jobs and protected the chosen-audio flow. That assessment principally judged whether the existing workflow could be trusted. It did not compare competing learning models or establish that saying the same words after a model improves an unfamiliar conversation. The separate product handoff posed that question, and E1/E2 were subsequently implemented and independently checked with local fixtures. The earlier investigations were useful within their mandates. Strategic discovery now has a functioning player and two low-cost experiments, so it can compare actual experiences before committing to new infrastructure. The main structural gap is the unit of practice. A timed phrase is the right unit for finding precise moments in a recording. A **speaking intention** such as clarifying a date or softening a refusal can be rehearsed alone, with a reference or with a partner. It need not become a lesson or add listening coverage. Choose the unit that suits the job and preserve the established player contract when crossing between them. The short current cues are a useful test. A new persisted exercise type, navigation rewrite or migration would add cost before that difference proves useful. ## Where shadowing helps and where another task is needed

Speaking a short distance behind a moving model is strict shadowing. Echo also supports listening followed by pause-and-repeat, and continuous listening with or without speaking. These activities place different demands on the learner. A transcript, a pause and replay make a sound or contrast easier to inspect. Reading and repeating a known line supply the words, syntax and intention. A different message removes that support. The next task should follow the owner’s aim rather than require every technique in every session.

| Aim | Use of a model | Complement and observation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pronunciation, word stress and articulation | Notice and repeat one chosen sound, stressed syllable or word that matters to a listener. Broad British IPA can cue a dictionary form. The selected voice supplies the actual utterance. | Identify the contrast by ear where useful. Put the word or an example of the sound in a new sentence. Can a willing listener recover the word or important detail? Check context and hearing conditions before attributing a misunderstanding to articulation. Keep matching a loved voice a separate preference. |
| Intonation, stress, pacing and connected speech | Hear where a speaker groups words and makes a contrast audible. Use a comfortable pause-and-repeat or play along if attention allows. | Practise two intentions with the same words, then carry one into an unrelated message. Ask which detail, stance or next action the listener heard. A natural pause or a slower pace can help. Judge the intended meaning rather than pitch range, speed, or how many consonants or vowels were reduced. |
| Expressive delivery and presentation | Notice one choice such as making reassurance audible or giving a brief signpost. | Use the choice for a different audience. Ask the listener to identify the stance and main point. A fresh follow-up samples repair and adapting to the audience. Echo can offer a scenario. A willing partner is the simplest source of a real reply. |
| Spontaneous fluency and sentence formulation | Repeating the gist can lower immediate planning load. A model supplies examples of phrasing and register. | Give a new explanation from a short intent, with no exact wording. Change one constraint or take an unexpected question. Observe whether the point gets across and how the speaker recovers after a hesitation. Solving a communicative breakdown matters more than speech rate. |
| Active vocabulary | Notice a collocation or phrase with a job, such as “on balance” when weighing a recommendation. Hearing and reading it provide recognition and access with support. | Hide the exact form and start with the communicative job on a later occasion. Try a different context before Replay. Record exact unaided retrieval, a fitting paraphrase and a prompted answer separately. A fitting alternative is a successful message even when the selected expression did not come to mind. |
| Vocal clarity, comfort, tone and energy | A comfortable example can illustrate phrasing or emphasis. A single voice remains a personal model among many British varieties. | Plan a short phrase and pause where helpful, or prepare silently. The speaker reports ease or additional effort. A listener can report what they understood. Stop with pain, strain or increasing effort. Assessing vocal health, resonance physiology or stamina calls for appropriate expertise and evidence beyond this practice. |
| Confidence and regular use | A familiar recording and private replay can make trying a turn more inviting. | Check whether the owner chooses to speak or use the route again on comparable real opportunities. Ask about ease and enjoyment separately. Declining, having no opportunity and leaving the outcome unobserved are different. |

For a practical example, listen to a voice contrasting the *draft* with the *final version*. Try the chosen emphasis once. Put the line away. Explain to a different teammate where a meeting has moved, then ask them to bring a document. The transfer question later is whether a **new** correction or request was clear. A separate same-wording pair can examine the effect of emphasis. Writing the correct date into a script, reading one line accurately or confirming a familiar clip sounds better answers a different question. ## Credible alternatives

| Direction | What materially changes | Benefit, cost and stopping condition |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Reference-first or media library** | Prioritise favourite voices, simple capture of a lawful highlight, precise Replay and straightforward Return. Use tools already available to practise a new message when wanted. | Lowest disruption and high autonomy. It can be the best strategy if the owner mainly wants to listen and imitate, or uses a paper cue happily. It leaves practice away from known words entirely to the owner. Favour it if same-time replay produces equally useful later messages and the owner prefers the route. |
| **Guided practice** | Offer one ready turn at open and a concrete end. Show a later challenge only when useful. A curated sequence can arrange an audible model, independent prompt and later recall. | Good candidate for empty-library and busy-day starts. Full daily programming requires editorial sequencing, evidence of continued use and a useful recommendation policy. An imposed path or cue of the day can crowd a preferred favourite. Start with one original situation and permit skipping. Stop if paper is equally available or returning users lose their place. |
| **Skills studio** | Start from a pronunciation, phrasing, vocabulary or communication task, with contrast-based practice and optional appropriate feedback. | Strong when the owner has a precise, recurring problem. Too many skill levels, scores and instructions before the first turn increase choice and upkeep. Test one listener-relevant contrast before proposing a catalogue or personalised scheduler. A recurring breakdown a trained coach can address is also a reason to use that outside resource. |
| **Two direct starts, with an optional bridge** | Keep the reference path and add an independent prompt that can be used immediately or after a chosen phrase. Let recall be a private check with fresh facts. | Best candidate for simultaneous prep and transfer problems. Added complexity is principally authored prompts, focus/navigation and the pause/return boundary. The current E1/E2 and paper let us isolate whether the product connection is worthwhile. Withdraw the expanded experience if it delays Resume, fails to improve starts or ends up as a checklist of obligatory steps. |
| **Off-app practice and pair work** | Use the desk card, a real conversation or a willing partner for one follow-up and repair. Introduce a tutor where individual phonetic or voice feedback warrants it. | Cheapest useful comparator and a stronger source of genuine interaction than a fixed prompt. Someone else’s availability can be the limiting factor. Keep this route if on-screen support adds little. Use a solo attempt with missing listener evidence when no partner is present. |
| **Automated feedback or a full AI coach** | Audio capture and a model infer words, grades, or questions. This could offer selected feedback when no person is available. | Requires a construct with demonstrable validity, an evaluation population, handling of valid alternatives and a reason to accept recording, privacy and recurring cost. Correct transcription or an accent benchmark score gives no assurance of natural intent, listener understanding or learning. Defer. Compare self-playback, a written cue and a willing listener first. |

Compare the two direct starts with the current reference-first flow and off-app practice. Keep original text, clear tasks and feedback choices few enough to understand without onboarding. Start by testing freely chosen activity and easy re-entry, then reconsider guided scheduling if return remains a problem. Anki’s recall-before-reveal pattern and Lute’s user-selected material are transferable ideas for one deliberate turn. Both leave the distinct problem of speaking appropriately for a new listener to the learner. ## Content worth practising with

The content promise is a short voice or situation the owner actually wants to revisit. Classify sources by the job they serve and the work per **usable** item, including search, authority, repair and device replay. Start with one or a handful. The strongest proof of a useful reference is repeat choice and better access to the chosen feature in a new message. A catalogue has ongoing editorial, rights and availability costs.

| Route | Effort, rights and suitability | Decision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Original fictional text and the owner's favourites | Text-only cues remove timing, rights search and import from the first attempt. Prepared favourites preserve personal interest and authentic phrasing. Existing playback and use permissions govern the favourite. | Start here. A short set of varied, useful intents can be authored by hand if the fixed cue soon repeats itself. A card is the reference for prep cost. |
| Official embed with authorised captions | The correct caption track and exact recording edit are necessary for bounded clips. A clip can become unavailable. The YouTube API discovery record exposes authenticated caption download scopes. Check track authorisation and redistribution rights before reuse. | Keep supported playback and compliant controls. Compare timing or correcting a *small* passage with a full lesson, and label approximate starts and ends. Use creator-provided or owned text. Offer another reference or text when media fails. [Caption method](https://developers.google.com/youtube/v3/docs/captions/download) · [API discovery declaration](https://github.com/googleapis/google-api-python-client/blob/main/googleapiclient/discovery_cache/documents/youtube.v3.json) |
| Owner-supplied audio with exact matching text or a whole utterance | Source-aligned SRT/VTT is reusable when the owner has it. One whole short file can use natural boundaries. Confirm permissions for use of the text, performance and recording. Private uploads reside under the existing owner scope. | Compare a supported native workflow and a chosen whole clip in a device player. If a recurring preparation problem remains, specify small audio-first creation with cue boundaries optional for whole-file play. Preserve imports, edits, marks and recovery. |
| One consented, natural model | Write an original short request, ask a willing speaker of a desired variety for one take, transcribe what was said and check audible edges on phone and headphones. A favourite may already supply the feature. Capture lawful recording/retention, approved uses, attribution and withdrawal separately for script, performance and master. | E3 off-app comparison is ready in principle and awaits a specific gap plus verified rights. Consider a second clip only with recurring added value. Keep rights evidence private and plan withdrawal for any later distribution. [E3 clearance and device check](../product/speaking-prompt-handoff.md#e3-f1-and-e3-f2--try-one-permissioned-reference-off-app) |
| Provider links and research corpora | Link to lawful public listening where the provider permits. A download option, British label or corpus licence rarely establishes the right *and* fit for an adapted exercise. VoxPopuli documents transcribed portions and marks its data CC0 while referring to the raw-footage notice. Parliament speeches suit a different register from short workplace corrections. | Read exact terms before selecting or distributing an asset. Review its speaker, task, naturalness and boundaries. Compare validated ready minutes on the same files against manual whole-clip editing. Keep output editable and review before publishing. Avoid bundling inference, proprietary data or extra cloud transfer merely to offer convenience. ## Discovery sequence and decisions

1. **Remove the preparation barrier for a turn.** Compare the current Home prompt, prepared Resume, a two-start wireframe and a desk card on comparable opportunities. End each with a similar unaided fictional update to the same kind of listener. Log time from deciding to practise to the first independent attempt, completion, avoidable interactions, abandoned starts and voluntary reuse. Separately time creating or repairing a new lesson. A wireframe can reveal misunderstanding and choice cost. Measure tap-to-ready and speech-start time on the actual app before claiming a device improvement. Prepared Resume must still require one activation to open the same lesson before Play. Compare large text, one-handed phone use, keyboard exit, interrupted or unavailable media and a privacy-locked cache.
2. **Try a bridge from hearing to a fresh message.** Choose one personally meaningful target and take a new unaided baseline. Alternate the same reference and equal total practice time with Replay-only versus replacing a repeat with one changed message. Use freshly matched facts, rotate condition order and avoid showing the later probe or intended answer in advance. On a later day before replay, ask for an unfamiliar update of comparable complexity. Give the optional listener the same question about changed detail and next action, without revealing the route. If the question is prominence, use fixed wording across intentions in a separate probe. A response to an unexpected follow-up is a separate outcome. Note ceiling, topic familiarity, fatigue and whether the observer knew the aim.
3. **Improve preparation only where it blocks a specific reference.** Try a favourite, an original cue and one rights-cleared whole native clip on-device if the owner actually misses that delivery. Compare total approved and checked preparation time, real replay, first and last words, repeat use and a later new message. If whole-clip preparation solves the bottleneck, review simple native creation before any alignment service or catalogue. Require reuse and a meaningful benefit to the intended task as well as lawful durable delivery before recommending a bundled sample.
4. **Decide how the owner returns.** Trial a new intention or one phrase retrieval on another ordinary day using paper, and ask what actually helped them remember to try. Expose a ready return in the app only if that solves a recurring access problem beyond the paper or favourite. Consider a small authored set before creating history, an exercise hierarchy or scheduling. The E4 card, silence and rest are usable without this work. [Existing private trial protocols](../product/speaking-prompt-handoff.md)

Agree what makes each comparison worth continuing before starting it. For a small owner decision, seek recurrence on different fresh prompts and voluntary use outside the first novel encounter. Set an acceptable preparation budget for an item with the owner before looking at content tools. If the routes tie, prefer the cheaper useful route or leave the result open. If the baseline is already at ceiling, choose a relevant harder intention or retire that target. If a listener is unavailable or a result is explained by unmatched tasks, keep that field unknown. If a flow takes longer than a prepared Resume or makes it harder to leave mid-turn, revise the interface before pursuing breadth. If only copied lines improve, keep that as evidence of rehearsal rather than continuing the transfer claim. Stop spoken rehearsal with discomfort or strain. Clinician-guided care is a separate path when concerns persist. [Voice care guidance](https://www.nidcd.nih.gov/health/taking-care-your-voice)

| Evidence type | Record only what helps a decision |
| --- | --- |
| Operation and access | Entry route, steps, delays, permission prompts, complete playback, retained position, immediate exit and ordinary recovery. Engineering can test these with synthetic fixtures. |
| Use and burden | Choosing a route again, preparation and repair effort, whether the speaking opportunity was available, abandoned or completed. Shorter setup and repeat visits are adoption observations. |
| Independent communication | Intended fact, whether the listener recovered it and next action on first hearing or after one repair, selected lexical form versus apt paraphrase, cue use. Store a private outcome label if useful. Leave listener outcomes unknown on a solo day. |
| Personal preference and ease | Owner’s report of enjoyment, comfort, fidelity to a chosen model or willingness to speak. A distinct accent can be intelligible and appropriate. |

No new response or voice data is needed for these comparisons. Use fictional prompts, voluntary short private notes and a willing listener only if the owner chooses. No names, meeting details, voice samples or symptom histories are necessary. Keep activity counters factual and under the existing contract. The results of these individual comparisons can guide a product decision. Claims about effect size, a practice dose, clinical change or generalisation to other speakers need different evidence.

## Research provenance and limits

Provider documentation, original public project files and publisher-maintained abstracts were inspected for this review. Publisher and institutional hosts for the pronunciation, spaced recall and fluency studies, the clinical and voice guidance pages, and Google’s detailed YouTube documentation returned HTTP 403 through the managed proxy. The records below distinguish what that access supports. No commercial speech tool was installed or given speech, and no learner or device trial was conducted in this workstream.

**Recommendation for approval.** Use the current E1 and E2 candidates, an off-app comparison and the existing library to test two direct starts and one optional changed-message bridge. Keep broader voice and reference experiments conditional on the owner’s preference, a specific missing example and appropriate care or rights. If there is a recurring benefit, use it to specify a smaller product change with the design, playback and QA specialists. Record inconclusive, equal or uncomfortable outcomes as well as positive ones. A credible roadmap can end an experiment and retain the part of Echo the owner already values.
